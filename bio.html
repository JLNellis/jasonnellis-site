01 · The longer story

Why behind
the career.

The part a résumé leaves out.

When I was 19, doctors found Stage 2B Hodgkin's lymphoma. I was at Northwestern, a theatre major, a lot more at home onstage than in an exam room. I did the chemo and the radiation and finished school anyway — then moved to Los Angeles instead of back to the East Coast, because by then playing it safe had a cost I could actually feel.

"Someday" stopped being a safe assumption that year. It never really came back.

That urgency turned into a pattern: find something worth building and get to it before the rest of the room realizes it's worth building. It took me through Hulu's early years, a company I co-founded that Facebook acquired, a stint building Live Shopping inside Meta as the team's first Founder in Residence, and eventually the Interim CEO seat at SuperBam.

None of it followed a strategy document. It followed a read on systems — which ones were about to break open, which ones needed someone to care more carefully than the people already in charge. I think in stories before slides, and I lead with the narrative. That frustrates the people who want a deck before they'll engage with the problem. It works on everyone else.

In April 2025 I moved the family to the south of France. Not a sabbatical, not a gesture — a bet that the life we'd been optimizing for in LA wasn't the one we actually wanted. I write, advise, and build from here now. Kit and Ace are the best evidence it's working.
